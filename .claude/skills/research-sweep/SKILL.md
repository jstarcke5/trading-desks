# Research sweep

Use before committing to an approach — not to gather links, but to constrain the space enough
that you stop generating ideas that were tried and abandoned years ago.

## The question that actually matters

Not "how is this done?" — the average answer is easy to find and is what everyone else already
did. Ask instead:

> **What separates masterful work here from merely competent work, and what specifically do the
> competent miss that stops them getting there?**

The gap between good and great is usually not more effort along the obvious axis. It is a
different axis entirely, chosen earlier and quietly.

## Method — multiple rounds, each seeded by the last

**Round 1 — map the consensus.** What does the standard approach look like? What is taught,
repeated, and assumed? Write it down explicitly; you need it as the thing to depart from.

**Round 2 — find the practitioners, not the explainers.** People who did it, ideally with
their own money or reputation exposed, and who published what went wrong. Weight anyone who
reports a failure far above anyone who reports a method. Failures are load-bearing; methods
are marketing.

**Round 3 — hunt the anti-patterns.** What does the graveyard look like? Which approaches
attract intelligent people repeatedly and fail for structural rather than execution reasons?
These are the expensive ones, because they *feel* like insight.

**Round 4 — find the constraint nobody states.** Every field has one — capital, latency,
approval, licence, data access, sample size, attention. It is usually why the retail version
of an institutional idea does not work, and it is usually left unsaid because insiders find it
too obvious to mention.

Run more rounds until new searches stop changing your answer. **Stopping when you have enough
to write something is not the same as stopping when the space is constrained.**

## Weighting what you find

- **Highest:** measured results with sample sizes, especially negative ones.
- **High:** first-hand accounts with specifics and admitted mistakes.
- **Low:** anything selling a method, a course, or a tool.
- **Zero:** backtests without costs, results without n, claims without a falsifier.

Recency matters asymmetrically — an edge published widely is an edge being competed away. If
you can read about it easily, so could everyone with more capital and better execution.

## Output

```
CONSENSUS:      what the standard approach is
MASTERFUL:      what the best do differently, and why it works
THE GAP:        what competent practitioners miss, specifically
ANTI-PATTERNS:  the graveyard, and why each one attracts smart people
THE CONSTRAINT: the unstated requirement that decides who this works for
SHOULD:         what this implies for us
SHOULD NOT:     what this rules out — usually the more valuable list
CONFIDENCE:     and what would change it
```

Finish by turning the sweep on itself: which of your own starting assumptions did this
invalidate? If the answer is none, you researched to confirm rather than to learn, and the
sweep did not do its job — run it again with the premise you were most sure of as the target.
